# Hexcore — mutable correction certificates and actionable evidence (rounds 16–19)

Status: designed finite examples and proofs, executable finite audits. Not a novelty claim, physical substrate, autonomous certificate synthesis, emergent reasoning, or universal law. Extends `hexcore_flux_guard_notes.md`. The target remains fixed and externally specified throughout. All costs here are unit steps unless explicitly stated otherwise; graph construction, certificate computation, validation, communication, and enforcement are not charged in the movement budgets.

## A. Individually valid certificates do not compose

The physical state is s in a finite set S with protected goal 0. A version v specifies a connected undirected graph on S; rho_v(s) is its shortest distance to 0. In each round an adversary chooses a version (including the current one), the controller observes it, and the controller takes one edge or waits. The version remains fixed until that action completes. An action costs one unit; handoff is free. The adversary can respond to the state and can freeze any admitted version indefinitely. There is no uncertainty about the physical state or version in sections A–C.

Three-state example: A has edges X–Y and Y–0; B has edges X–Y and X–0. At X, A assigns rank 2 and B rank 1; at Y the ranks are reversed. The adversary presents A at X and B at Y. Moving along the only edge at either location reduces the active rank by 1, but the following handoff raises it by 1. Waiting cannot improve the worst-case outcome. Both frozen graphs have diameter 2, yet there is no finite sure correction deadline. This also reproduces the previous moving-maze construction from the certificate-transfer perspective; it is not a separate new theorem about all dynamic graphs.

## B. Charge upward revisions to a finite reserve

For a valid handoff at state s, let delta = max(0, rho_new(s) - rho_old(s)). Maintain nonnegative integer credit b in an external ledger; admit the switch only when delta <= b, then subtract delta. No replenishment occurs during a correction episode. A rank-decreasing move is always available because the active rank is an exact distance. With Phi = rho_v(s)+b, the handoff cannot increase Phi; the subsequent descending step decreases it by at least one. Hence a rank-following policy reaches the goal in at most rho_initial(s)+b_initial actions. The exact distance is unnecessary for sufficiency; any nonnegative integer rank with zero only at the goal and a selectable descending step is sufficient. The current version must remain a permitted option, or the guard may deadlock.

For the three-state example starting in A at X, the bound b+2 is tight: after the initial move to Y, the adversary spends one unit on each upward rank reset until credit is exhausted. Then the next move must be to the goal under the same version. A wait cannot improve the bound. An exact minimax attractor verifies b=0..20. Refilling one unit per descending rank step, capped by the initial credit, allows perpetual oscillation for each b>=1 tested. It pays back the entire progress, destroying the strict decline of Phi. If refunds for downward *handoffs* are allowed instead, they may be paid up to the actual rank decrease without increasing Phi; this is distinct from refunding the progress of actions. That variant follows algebraically, not from an additional execution.

## C. Direct deadline guard and exact maximality under freeze availability

An alternative external ledger stores remaining movement budget d, decremented after every move or wait. A candidate new graph is admissible precisely if its exact shortest distance at the current state is <=d. If an old graph was already certified, at least one choice exists. For any admitted graph, one shortest-path step leaves a state with a route of length at most d-1 in that graph. Hence induction guarantees success within d under arbitrary sequences of admitted rewires. The guard does not require a common rank over physical states; the combined state with remaining budget has a well-founded deadline. It may allow a different correction path at each step.

In this specific game, the admission rule is pointwise maximally permissive: if one admits a graph with exact distance >d, the environment can hold that graph fixed for all d moves, and even its best path misses the deadline. This necessity relies on the adversary being allowed to freeze the admitted graph, unit costs, and exact distances (or exact optimal costs). If the graph is guaranteed to change helpfully later, a high frozen distance may not be an obstruction. If a certificate is merely an upper bound rather than an exact distance, rejecting a large bound is sound as a conservative rule, not proof the graph is infeasible. A known cost c of switching can be accounted for by requiring c+rho_new(s)<=d and spending c at the switch; no claim of free verification is implied.

The no-refund credit rule in B is more conservative than this direct deadline rule in general. For exact shortest-distance ranks and unit correction actions, the signed reserve b=d-rho_v(s) is equivalent to the direct deadline rule, allowing slack released by a shorter handoff to be reused; the deadline never resets. The immutable object is an external episode contract (goal, admissible costs, observation/control timing, remaining deadline and enforcement), not a particular topology or rank. This does not make the contract itself self-modifiable without a higher-level specification. A system cannot meaningfully guarantee arbitrary future redefinitions of its own success condition.

### Executed audit
`hexcore_rank_transfer.py` uses BFS for exact frozen distances, an independent finite-horizon minimax recursion for guarded reachability and forced-freeze controls, and a finite-state attractor for the credit game. Across 200 seeded random graph families (3–9 vertices, 2–5 versions): 7,497 non-goal location/deadline states, 75,347 candidate handoffs from certified old versions, 71,007 admitted (51,318 with changed version), and 4,340 rejected. Every accepted handoff has a winning move under the guard; every rejected one fails under the freeze control. Credit games with caps 0,1,3 are audited for all states. Two-mode visibility cases check horizons 1..64. 187,433 assertions, zero failures. Generated frequencies are descriptive, not estimates of real architectures.

## D. Rank existence versus actionable correction

At a non-goal state X, modes A and B both have exact rank 1. Action a succeeds in A and self-loops in B; action b succeeds in B and self-loops in A. The mode may be disclosed, hidden and constant, or hidden and chosen anew each round. Goal arrival is observable.

- Disclosed before action and held through it: exact worst-case 1 action.
- Hidden but constant: exact worst-case 2 actions, because a failure reveals which other action to use.
- Hidden and adversarially refreshed: no deterministic finite worst-case guarantee, despite all ranks being identical and valid. For each deterministic history-dependent policy the adversary can anticipate its next action and choose the other mode. The finite-horizon recurrence confirms this through 64 horizons; the inductive argument covers all finite horizons. This is not a claim that randomization cannot give almost-sure success (with independent fair action choice and an adversary not observing the current coin, it can). Sure bounded-time success is the criterion here.

This is a quantifier distinction: for each mode there exists a progress action does not entail there exists an observation-conditioned policy that always chooses one. In a general partial-observation game the certificate must be about the actual information state and allowed adversarial timing. The appropriate standard comparator is observation-based reachability games, not a rank with the true hidden mode supplied to the controller.

## E. Exact information cost of selecting a valid witness

Define m hidden modes with nonempty sets G_i of actions that reach the goal in one move. The controller is given a message from a trusted encoder that sees the current mode, and selects one action from its message. Wrong actions self-loop. The mode is held fixed until the action completes. Let tau be the size of a smallest set of actions hitting every G_i. Then the minimum number of distinct message labels is exactly tau; minimum fixed-width message length is ceil(log2 tau) bits.

Necessity: the controller's actions for all usable labels form a hitting set (each mode must have some label yielding a valid action). Sufficiency: choose a minimum hitting set and, for each mode, transmit the index of one member lying in G_i. The result does not require identifying the full mode; it requires discriminating sufficiently to select a valid action. The cost of computing the hitting set or generating the message is not counted. With distinct unique valid actions for m modes, tau=m even though every mode has the same rank 1; the number of necessary bits grows as ceil(log2 m). If the mode can change adversarially after the report, full identity of the old mode is not enough; the witness must remain valid through the action or a suitable common action must exist. This is a special finite set-cover/hitting-set instance of observation design, not a general new communication-complexity result.

`hexcore_certificate_visibility.py` independently enumerates hitting sets and partitions of modes into message classes. It checks all 343 assignments of nonempty good-action subsets to 3 modes with 3 actions: 169 need one label, 168 need two, 6 need three. It also checks 1000 seeded cases with 2–7 modes and actions; label counts 1:310, 2:622, 3:67, 4:1. Unique-action families verified by enumeration for m<=7 and by the proof for larger m. Seven stale-message finite checks (2..8 modes). 8,320 assertions, zero failures.

## F. Rediscovery and claim boundaries

- Podelski & Rybalchenko, *A Complete Method for the Synthesis of Linear Ranking Functions* (2004), https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-24622-0_20 : established ranking/termination machinery. The deadline guard is an elementary application of well-founded ranking and backward induction, not a universal law.
- Amortized potential accounting: https://tanktechnology.github.io/CLRS-Lean/CLRSLean/FourthEdition/Chapter_16/Section_16_1_Amortized_Framework/ . The proof in B uses the standard potential telescoping argument.
- Berwanger et al., *Strategy construction for parity games with imperfect information* (2010), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0890540110001070 ; Chatterjee, Doyen & Henzinger, *A survey of partial-observation stochastic parity games* (2013), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10703-012-0164-2 . Established observation-based strategy theory and quantifier discipline. Our two-mode example is a minimal special case; we do not claim belief states suffice for all stochastic or partial-observation games.

The practical next experiment is not to add yet another internal supervisor. It is to compare an externally enforced deadline and atomic witness channel, a timed freeze/dwell intervention, and standard observation-based controller synthesis in an actual mutable graph substrate. Charge storage, validation, information access, update and enforcement overhead. Define a real correction target beyond arriving at an anchor. Measure how much reconfiguration remains feasible under the guard; never interpret the fraction of admitted edits as intelligence. No implementation of the full medium or empirical superiority has been established. No repository change was made.
