# Hexcore developmental law discovery (DEV-05–08)

Executed exploratory finite program-induction experiment; not an artificial-life or self-modifying medium. Python 3.10+ and NumPy. From this directory run `python law_discovery.py` then `python ambiguity.py`. The protocols document the supplied machinery, controls, metrics and limitations. The second protocol is a post-result audit.

## Results

The solver enumerated 41,916 arithmetic combinations, retaining 2,280 distinct clipped profiles on n=2..64. The precomputation used a scalar-operation upper proxy of 2,640,708, 287,280 bytes for the final int16 profile table and 31,639 UTF-8 bytes of descriptions; this excludes Python objects, temporary raw profiles and other storage. It took ~0.325 s on this run. Five root-split demonstrations (two fixed, three chosen) yielded the exact intended profile in all six deliberately chosen expressible worlds. On 48 larger sizes (17..64), the active solver reconstructed all 48 trees in each of those worlds, compared with 31.01/48 for the same library with random queries, 17.83 for sequential queries, and 25.33 for a simple rational-fraction fit with the same active observations. All six active circuits at n=17 agreed with the reference over all 131,072 inputs. The fraction fit exactly matches three of the six worlds. It is cheaper and sufficient for them. Active querying costs more: mean 9,947 candidate comparisons plus entropy histogram visits vs ~3,995 consistency comparisons for passive; these counters do not equate to actual compute or energy. Per-world fitting time including ambiguity bookkeeping was ~0.954 ms vs 0.335 ms. The upfront library is shared; its cost must be charged for a fresh learner. No demonstrated lifetime compute advantage over optimized synthesis.

The two target laws outside the finite language (sqrt and log2) give 0 and 1 exact held-out trees respectively for the active solver; two threshold-shift aliases each give 0/48. The active solver correctly predicts all six expressible profiles but the five observations uniquely identify only two within the library. The other four retain multiple compatible profiles. Successful prediction is not proof of identifiability.

## Exact audit

Group the 2,280 distinct profiles by the vector of root splits at every permitted training size (4..16; sizes 2 and 3 are fixed by clipping). There are 1,521 classes, 1,342 singleton classes, and 938 profiles in non-singleton classes; the largest class contains 173 distinct profiles. All distinct profiles within a class differ somewhere on n=17..64. For a uniform prior over this finite library, even unrestricted access to every training observation cannot exceed 1521/2280 = 66.71% exact profile recovery. This is a prior- and library-dependent ceiling, not an empirical probability of discovering a natural law.

Three designed worlds (thirds, thirds-then-halves, thirds-then-quarters) agree at every training size and disagree pairwise at every held-out size. Every adaptive observation policy restricted to training sizes receives identical evidence. For each held-out size, any predictor's sum of success probabilities over the three worlds is at most one. Thus mean root accuracy under a uniform prior across those worlds is at most 1/3, and at least one world has accuracy at most 1/3. This holds even for randomized predictors or unlimited training queries. The solution is not a cleverer learner under the same observation contract, but new evidence or additional justified restrictions. This is standard identifiability/active-learning reasoning.

## Limits

Targets are hand-designed and in the same source file as the evaluator; learner functions receive only queried split values via a simulated oracle, not an adversarial isolation boundary. Demonstrations provide stronger information than earlier binary success feedback. Grammar, clipping, max expression size and recursive interpretation are supplied. Finite profile equivalence does not establish equivalence for unbounded n. This is conventional enumerative symbolic induction with active experiments, not invention of a new learning algorithm, emergent reasoning, a general developmental law, or a P vs NP result. No repository changes are made by these scripts.
