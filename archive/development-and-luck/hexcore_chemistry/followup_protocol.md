# Follow-up: what exactly should be inherited?
Post-hoc diagnostic, designed after the initial negative result and before execution of this follow-up. Use random-overwrite template and ordinary subtree arms; 24 seeds per arm, same four-input training and five-input targets as initial test. All prepared warm conditions receive phase-two budget minus a conservative preparation charge. All include the cost of phase one in lifetime comparisons. Compare to ordinary cold at B and 2B. No adaptive tuning.

Conditions: unmodified inherited pool; portable (random injective variable relabeling, same shape and length); leaf-scrambled (same shape and length, independent fresh variable at each leaf); random shape (same gate count, random tree and leaves); half fresh turnover (replace half the inherited pool with five-input food); small fragments (each inherited term contributes one uniformly sampled subtree <= 7 gates, then random injective relabeling; condition on the resulting fragment sizes); matched random small fragments (random trees of those same sizes, same preparation budget and fresh variable set). For direct comparability, paired conditions share phase-one pools. Charge 2 times sum of old and new token counts for any preparation, a conservative simple proxy for these linear transformations; actual time separately, including preparation. Subtree discovery scans every old token once; sampling and random tree construction are supplied operations. Preparation acts outside chemistry, not emergent. No target labels.

Primary diagnostics: portable vs inherited (access to new input); portable vs leaf-scrambled and random shape (does inherited structure help beyond size?); small fragments vs matched random small fragments (does accumulated organization help beyond retaining smaller components?); all against cold 2B (lifetime value). Report exact target hits and intervals. No new architecture claim; this is a test of transferable structural bias.
