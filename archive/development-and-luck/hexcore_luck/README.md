# Target-blind accidental discovery — November 28, 2026

Exploratory tests of whether earlier blind discoveries can become reusable operations in larger computational worlds. No solution to P versus NP, universal-law claim, autonomous reasoning or new learning mechanism is established. Run `python experiment.py`, then `python followup.py` (standard library only; timings vary). The first and follow-up protocols were written before their respective runs. Results include individual seeds, fixed target banks, summary counts and paired approximate intervals. The follow-up is prompted by the first outcome, not independent preregistration.

## Outcome

- In the first 512-target structured bank, accidental inherited templates improve coverage over a flat search given equal lifetime primitive budget: 124.34 -> 140.16 at four inputs, 1.78 -> 7.00 at five inputs. A systematic library obtains 148.00 and 7.53. At five inputs the inherited/systematic difference is not clear from the paired interval.
- Total distinct full-support functions fall in every seed at both arities (e.g. five inputs 6241.19 -> 4266.81).
- On new seeds and two new 1024-target banks, the 50% inherited-template-call policy increases mixed-generator hits but decreases NAND-only-generator hits at both arities. At five inputs: mixed 8.19 -> 14.09, NAND 160.84 -> 83.31, full-support 6236.22 -> 4377.22. Paired intervals quantify sampling variation across seeds, not uncertainty over arbitrary target distributions.
- The 0%-macro storage control distinguishes some loss due to allocating memory to library code. Further losses with use of the library suggest the search distribution matters as well. Rates are probabilities per proposal, not per primitive gate, and template costs vary. The sweep is exploratory; no rate wins on every metric. The systematic-prehistory control matches or exceeds accidental transfer on the mixed banks.

## Accounting and scope

All expanded NAND formula gates are charged, including intermediate gates which enter the common reservoir and external evaluation. The logical storage budget charges two operand words per macro gate and three metadata words per template; it is NOT a byte-accurate allocation. Python sets, lists, duplicated references (including the enumeration arm's pool), recursion, selection, archive maintenance, target generation and scoring incur additional overhead. Historical formula search uses cached truth tables; 800 primitive operations is not total work. A 32-bit truth-table calculation is a packed evaluation of all assignments, not one physical gate. Timing is measured but noisy and includes different bookkeeping; the protocols did not match wall-clock time. The macro library is generated within a supplied grammar; it does not invent a new computational substrate. The standard-library comparator includes AND, OR, XOR, XNOR, mux, and other simple expressions but, contrary to the illustrative list in the first protocol, does not explicitly include majority. The systematic future enumerator is a simple bounded pair enumeration, not an optimal circuit synthesis baseline. No target is consulted by the search policy. In the first experiment, hit counting is in the observer's record routine; it does not affect any subsequent action. In the follow-up, all hit scoring occurs after search.

## Interpretation

Let p_A(x) denote the probability that an exploration strategy A encounters a function x by budget B. Expected distinct coverage is sum_x p_A(x). Expected coverage of a fixed target set T is sum_{x in T} p_A(x). Any strategy that lowers the former while raising the latter must reduce discovery probability for some other functions. This elementary identity is not a new universal law. The experiments illustrate a trade-off: reusable structure can increase discovery in aligned environments while narrowing exploration elsewhere. Accidents are not intrinsically beneficial; retention and composition change the probability distribution of subsequent accidents. No causal claim about particular lucky templates is made.
