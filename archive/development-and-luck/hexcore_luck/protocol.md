# Target-blind transfer of accidental operations — exploratory protocol

Written before running the experiment. Question: can randomly encountered small operations be retained as parameterized templates and accelerate later discovery, without a target-specific reward or treating a macro as a free gate?

- Historical world: 3 Boolean variables, NAND only. 800 random pair reactions; exact truth tables are retained with the shortest encountered formula (expanded cost <= 12). Choose up to 12 distinct templates that depend on at least two inputs, have 2–9 NAND gates, uniformly from the eligible archive. No future targets enter this process.
- Future worlds: 4 and 5 Boolean variables; 32 seeds (14000..14031). Every arm emits one gate result per primitive NAND invocation. Budget 12,000 invocations including all macro internals, with an additional 800 for fresh equal-lifetime controls. A 512-word abstract storage budget: reusable template code charged two operand words per gate plus three metadata words per macro; remaining words are available for unique truth tables in the reservoir. Inputs and external scoring are common and excluded. The reservoir uses random eviction, no task fitness. Base operand drawn from primitive inputs with probability .4, otherwise from retained results. Each macro call uses three sampled operands and executes its expanded NAND formula. In macro arms half of calls use macros; all gate outputs, including intermediate gates, enter the reservoir and external evaluation. Stop exactly at the primitive gate budget. Count prehistory separately.
- Arms: random primitive-only; random primitive-only equal lifetime; inherited random templates; standard supplied NAND-expanded library (AND, OR, XOR, implication, majority, mux, etc.); a systematic semantic enumeration library obtained with equal 800-gate prehistory; and systematic unordered pair enumeration at future arity (bounded first-512 unique results, no replacement). Systematic future arm gets equal lifetime budget. All arms use the same input basis. No target feedback. Include 12,000-gate future comparison for all where possible; equal lifetime controls are stronger in access to new inputs.
- Evaluation: distinct truth tables ever emitted; count functions that depend on all variables (exact); independent, fixed target bank of 512 distinct full-support functions per arity generated using random compositions of AND/OR/XOR/NOT/majority/mux; each must depend on all variables. The target generator is intentionally structured and is not evidence for arbitrary environments. Also evaluate coverage of the entire 4-input function space (65,536), not just the target bank. At 1000, 4000, 12000 primitive gates record cumulative hits. Record code size, reservoir cap, gate count, runtime. Compare paired mean differences and standard errors. No hidden-target tuning.
- Defeat: if inherited templates fail to improve over primitive-only equal-lifetime, no evidence of amortized advantage in that world; if conventional library or enumeration matches, absorb into ordinary program search/reuse. A positive result only shows a scoped search-distribution effect, not new reachability under equal execution cost or a universal law. Storage words are a declared abstraction, not actual resident bytes. Timing excludes independent target generation and scoring of full-support counts; Python overhead and evaluator instrumentation matter.
